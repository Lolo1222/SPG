Diffusion-based large language models (dLLMs), particularly masked diffusion models like LLaDA \cite{nie2025large}, have emerged as a compelling paradigm for generative modeling. By iteratively refining sequences through a denoising process, dLLMs offer a non-autoregressive alternative that breaks the sequential dependency of traditional autoregressive (AR) models. This paradigm shift promises significant advantages: closed-source models like Mercury and Gemini Diffusion achieve over an order of magnitude speed-up in generation compared to AR counterparts while maintaining comparable quality \cite{labs2025mercury}, and open-weight models such as LLaDA demonstrate competitive performance parity with equivalently sized AR baselines on standard benchmarks \cite{zhu2025llada}.

To unlock the full potential of these models, particularly in instruction following and complex reasoning, aligning them with human preferences via Reinforcement Learning (RL), such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), is crucial \cite{ouyang2022training, shao2024deepseekmath}. However, applying RL to dLLMs presents unique and severe challenges compared to the AR paradigm.
In AR models, the probability of a sequence is exactly computed via the chain rule. In contrast, due to the iterative, non-sequential generation process of dLLMs, the exact log-likelihood requires marginalizing over high-dimensional latent trajectories, which is computationally intractable \cite{zhao2025d1, wang2025spg}. Consequently, policy gradient estimation must rely on surrogates, typically the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO).

This reliance on ELBO approximation introduces critical bottlenecks in both \textit{efficiency} and \textit{stability}. \textbf{First, the computational cost is prohibitive.} The RL algorithm itself requires extensive sampling, and computing the ELBO estimator further demands multiple forward passes to average out noise. When combined with the inherent iterative nature of diffusion, the total training overhead becomes unmanageable. \textbf{More critically, the inherent high variance of the ELBO estimator destabilizes RL training.} In algorithms relying on importance sampling (e.g., GRPO/PPO), the policy update depends on likelihood ratios. Small estimation errors in the denominator can induce massive bias in the policy gradient target, leading to gradient mismatch and unstable training dynamics \cite{tang2025wd1}. There is, therefore, an urgent need for an algorithm that can simultaneously reduce the time overhead and the estimation variance to enable stable RL on dLLMs.

To address these challenges, we adopt a unified perspective on the dLLM generation process, viewing it as a trajectory within a \textbf{spatio-temporal lattice}. In this lattice structure, the \textit{spatial} dimension spans the discrete token indices of the sequence, while the \textit{temporal} dimension captures the iterative progression of decoding steps. We observe that standard sampling algorithms blindly traverse this entire lattice, performing redundant computations. Drawing inspiration from **Semi-Offline RL** \cite{chen2023semi}, which theoretically grounds the trade-off between exploration capability and optimization cost, we propose **Spatio-Temporal Mask Scheduling (STMS)**. STMS prunes the lattice in both dimensions by rigorously applying the principles of semi-offline learning:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Spatial Pruning (Restricted Exploration via Static Data):} Echoing the semi-offline strategy of mixing static data with dynamic generation, we utilize dataset priors to fix a subset of "anchor" tokens along the spatial dimension before RL sampling begins. This effectively restricts the exploration space to a high-confidence sub-manifold, leveraging quality data to guide the policy—similar to supervised fine-tuning—rather than relying solely on the model's unconstrained sampling.

\item \textbf{Temporal Pruning (Partially Observable Optimization):} Following the "Partially Observable MDP" formulation in semi-offline RL \cite{chen2023semi}, which reduces optimization cost by limiting state observation, we treat the fine-grained denoising steps in the late diffusion stage as unobserved latent variables. By performing an early-exit via one-step decoding, we optimize the policy based on a partially observed temporal trajectory. This significantly reduces the computational horizon while maintaining sufficient signal for effective gradient estimation.

\end{enumerate}

We provide a theoretical analysis demonstrating that STMS achieves a "win-win" outcome: it significantly accelerates trajectory sampling by reducing the effective computational volume on the lattice and, crucially, **provably reduces the variance of the ELBO estimator**. By eliminating stochastic terms in both spatial and temporal dimensions, STMS enables more stable and efficient reinforcement learning for dLLMs.
